Observations
  • Aqua HK spent HK$8,397 on Meta and Meta recorded 16 schedules, about HK$525 each, against 10 schedules on HK$6,609 the week before. Do not read either number as a CPA. The Meta Schedule conversion fires from the same website confirmation page as the GA4 booking event and both have been under-recording since 15 to 16 October.
  • Dim Sum Library spent HK$1,038 for 2 schedules, flat on the week in both spend and schedules. Two schedules against 132 SevenRooms website bookings is a measurement artefact, not a result, and DSL’s single prospecting campaign is the whole account.
  • Across the eight Hong Kong accounts Meta recorded 52 schedules against 24 the week before, which is the clearest single sign that the tag is recovering: it more than doubled while spend was flat at about HK$42,800. It has not recovered fully. Treat Meta bookings and Meta CPA for this week as directional only.

Observations
  • Aqua HK Google ran HK$8,004 for about 43 booking conversions at roughly HK$187 CPA, against 70 conversions on HK$9,958 the week before. Read that fall with care: Google conversions settle upward for two to three weeks, and last week’s own W38 figure restated from 69 to 70 and its walk-in total from 664 to 1,023 in this run alone. The current week is provisional.
  • Dim Sum Library Google delivered about 61 conversions on HK$1,517 at roughly HK$25 CPA, the most efficient account in the estate by a wide margin and up from 60 on HK$1,707. HK$211 of brand spend produced 44.9 of those 61 conversions. That is existing name demand being harvested, not new reach, and it is the clearest case in Hong Kong for holding brand flat and putting new money where organic cannot reach.
  • Brand search carried the volume at both venues: 22.4 of Aqua’s 42.8 conversions came from Search > Brand on HK$797 of spend, and 44.9 of Dim Sum Library’s 60.9 from its brand campaign on HK$211. Google fires its own tag rather than a GA4 import at both venues, so this is the one booking signal in Hong Kong that is not broken.
